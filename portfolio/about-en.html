My career in three stages

My name is Jianqiu Gai. I’m a software engineer with over thirteen years of experience. I started with iOS development, and over time, my work has grown to include smart devices, security SDKs, and full-stack development.

I would describe my career in three stages. The first was building e-commerce apps. The second was connecting apps with smart devices and building reusable SDKs and modules. The third, and most recent, is working on security and using AI coding tools to deliver complete systems.

Stage one: e-commerce and the user experience

In the first stage, I worked at Tuniu and Yoho. Both roles were closely related to e-commerce, although they served different users. Tuniu focused on travel, while Yoho focused on fashion.

At Tuniu, I worked on the iPad shopping experience and travel detail pages. This helped me build a strong foundation in iOS development, especially Objective-C, page behavior, and the app lifecycle.

At Yoho, I continued working on shopping features and also worked with React Native, live streaming, WeChat mini programs, and native A/B testing. These experiences gave me a broader view of mobile development and different ways to deliver features.

The most important thing I learned during this stage was to think from the user’s point of view. A feature needs to do more than work correctly. It should also be clear, smooth, and easy to use. That focus on user experience is still part of how I work today. I bring that same attention to pixel-perfect UI details, responsive interactions and fluid workflows.

Stage two: smart devices and reusable SDKs

In the second stage, I moved into smart devices, first at Tuya Smart and then at Huami.

At Tuya, I worked on commercial SDKs for smart devices. My work included helping devices connect to Wi-Fi, maintaining device-related features, and supporting apps built for business customers. I also took part in device account-linking work related to Alexa and Google Home.

This was a different kind of challenge from building app pages. A device may go offline, a connection may fail, or a task may finish later than expected. I had to think carefully about connection states, error handling, and compatibility.

I also learned to think about the developers who use an SDK. The interface should be easy to understand, the behavior should be predictable, and the documentation should help them solve problems.

At Huami, I worked on Mi Fit and Zepp, using Swift and SwiftUI. My work covered health features, watch faces, the lab section, and the world clock, together with Bluetooth device integration.

I also led a project to divide device features into reusable modules. Besides writing code, I broke down tasks, coordinated resources, and followed the delivery schedule.

This stage helped me move from building individual features to designing components that could support different devices and products.

Stage three: security and AI-assisted full-stack delivery

From April 2022 to October 2026, I worked on an on-site project at the State Grid Electric Power Research Institute, focusing on mobile security, secure communication and, more recently, full-stack development.

On the mobile side, I maintained secure-access SDKs and developed an iOS security monitoring SDK. After a vendor stopped providing support, I independently developed a core security library. It detects risks such as jailbroken devices, debugging, code injection, and changes to important system functions.

For me, security is not only about detecting a risk. It is also about keeping normal apps stable. I test both risky cases and normal devices, investigate false alarms, and check what happens when the app moves between the foreground and background.

I also worked on secure network connections. For example, I investigated failures when a phone switches between Wi-Fi and mobile data, or when several reconnect attempts happen at the same time. This often requires tracing a problem from Objective-C down to C or C++.

A recent project shows how my role has expanded beyond mobile development. I independently delivered an API security monitoring project using AI coding tools, mainly Claude Code and Codex.

The original estimate was about six months, and I delivered the project in three weeks. My work covered the data processing pipeline, changes to an existing web application, testing, and deployment to an internal network.

The system collects network data, processes API requests, and presents API assets and security risks through a web interface. I used Java and Flink for data processing, Elasticsearch for storage and search, and Docker for deployment.

AI helped me write routine code, explore unfamiliar parts of the stack, and prepare test scripts and documentation. My responsibility was to define the tasks, make design decisions, review the results, and verify the actual system behavior.

For example, a chart can look correct while showing the wrong numbers. I had to trace the data through the system and fix issues such as duplicate counts and time-zone differences. That experience taught me to measure progress by working results and reliable data.

I have also taken on mobile team lead and deputy R&D team lead responsibilities. I enjoy staying close to the code while helping the team organize work and explain technical problems clearly.

What I bring to my next team

Looking back, these three stages connect naturally. E-commerce taught me to care about users. Smart devices and SDKs taught me to build reliable connections and reusable components. Security and full-stack work taught me to take responsibility for a complete system.

My strongest foundation is still iOS and native development. At the same time, I can work across the app, the SDK, and the backend when a project needs it. I’m comfortable learning new tools, and I use AI to support that work while staying responsible for the quality of the result.

In my next role, I’d like to bring together my mobile experience, problem-solving skills, and AI-assisted development approach to help the team build useful and reliable products.

I would be happy to discuss any of these projects in more detail.
